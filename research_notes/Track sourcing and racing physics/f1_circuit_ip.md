# F1 circuit IP and licensing risk

**Research cutoff and method.** Practical IP-risk research, not legal advice. Sources were checked on **2026-10-14**; where a page did not state a publication date, this is recorded as “not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.” The key distinction is between (a) a physical circuit and its publicly observable facts, (b) a particular map/CAD/database expression of those facts, (c) trademarks/false-association risk, and (d) game assets/code/data.

## 1. Physical layouts, CAD, maps, and datasets

### Takeaway

There is no single global rule that makes every real-world circuit outline automatically copyright-protected, but an exact recreation is not a clean “facts are free” case. Copyright generally protects expression rather than ideas, procedures, methods, or mathematical concepts; at the same time, authoritative sources treat maps, technical drawings, databases, and architecture as potentially protected. Formula 1’s own current guidance expressly identifies “circuit outlines” as circuit-owner IP to be cleared with the owner. For a non-commercial college game, the conservative risk position is therefore **original geometry inspired by racing design principles**, not an exact, recognizable recreation copied or traced from a map, CAD plan, SVG, simulator, or game.

### Cited Findings

- WIPO’s copyright overview lists “computer programs, databases,” “architecture,” “maps,” and “technical drawings” among works commonly protected, while stating: **“Copyright protection extends only to expressions, and not to ideas, procedures, methods of operation or mathematical concepts as such.”** (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [WIPO, Copyright](https://www.wipo.int/copyright/en/)

- WIPO’s copyright FAQ repeats the same boundary and separately says copyright commonly covers “maps and technical drawings.” It also says copyright generally arises automatically and that, in general, authorization is needed before using another person’s protected work; public availability on the Internet is not the same as public-domain status. (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [WIPO, Frequently Asked Questions: Copyright](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright)

- The U.S. Copyright Office states: **“Copyright does not protect facts, ideas, systems, or methods of operation, although it may protect the way these things are expressed.”** The same official FAQ says copyright protects original works including “computer software” and “architecture.” (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [U.S. Copyright Office, Copyright in General FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html)

- Formula 1’s current official IP guidelines say that, in addition to marks, Formula 1 companies own or have exclusive commercial rights in other IP connected to the championship, including **“Results and Timing data,” “Statistics,” “Official Artworks, Graphics, Assets and Textures,”** and written content. The same page then says: **“If you would like to enquire about licensing any of the rights of the circuits’ intellectual property rights, such as circuit outlines, please contact the owner of the IP directly.”** (The page lists event titles “as of October 2026”; accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- The current Formula 1 legal-notices page says Formula One Digital Media Limited has the exclusive right to commercialise the FIA Formula One World Championship in all media, and that site content includes live timing, historical race data, photographs, images, illustrations, text, video, and other materials protected by copyright, database rights, trademarks, and/or other IP rights. It also prohibits reproducing, modifying, adapting, or preparing derivative works from site material without the stated permission. (Publication date not stated; page footer shows © 2003–2026; accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Legal Notices](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/legal-notices.7egvZU48hzrypubGBNcQKt)

- UK Intellectual Property Office guidance says a commissioned work normally remains owned by the creator unless ownership is agreed in writing, and specifically says for student work that copyright normally remains with the student unless a college/university contract assigns it or extracts a licence. (Published 2014-08-19; accessed 2026-10-14.) [GOV.UK / IPO, Ownership of copyright works](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works)

- UK IPO guidance describes non-commercial research/private-study and teaching exceptions as specific, limited exceptions requiring fair dealing and, in relevant cases, acknowledgement. It says educational-establishment performance/showing exceptions apply to an audience connected with the establishment, and its fair-dealing analysis considers market effect and whether the amount taken was reasonable. (Published 2014-06-12; last updated 2021-01-04; accessed 2026-10-14.) [GOV.UK / IPO, Exceptions to copyright](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright)

- The FIA’s official organisation page describes the FIA as the governing body for world motor sport; its regulations page lists current FIA Formula One World Championship regulations and document publication dates in 2026. These pages establish the FIA’s regulatory role, but they do **not** by themselves grant permission to copy circuit geometry, FIA marks, or F1 assets. (Publication dates for the web pages not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [FIA, Organisation](https://www.fia.com/organisation); [FIA, Formula One regulations](https://www.fia.com/regulation/category/110)

- OpenStreetMap’s official copyright page says OSM data is licensed under the Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL), and summarizes the permission as: **“You are free to copy, distribute, transmit and adapt our data, as long as you credit OpenStreetMap and its contributors. If you alter or build upon our data, you may distribute the result only under the same license.”** It separately requires an attribution notice and a link/notice making the ODbL available. (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [OpenStreetMap, Copyright and License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

- The ODbL legal text says it licenses rights in the database, including database copyright and database rights, but **“does not cover the copyright over the Contents independent of this Database.”** It says individual contents may have separate copyright, patent, data-protection, privacy, personality, or other rights. It grants worldwide, royalty-free use including commercial use, subject to the license conditions; public use of a Produced Work requires a notice that the content was obtained from the database, while public conveyance of a derivative database triggers license/notice/share-alike obligations. (ODbL v1.0; publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [Open Data Commons, ODbL v1.0 legal code](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)

- OSM Foundation’s licence page records that data extracted after September 2012 is licensed under ODbL 1.0. The OSM copyright page also warns contributors not to add data from copyrighted sources without permission and says OSM cannot provide a free-of-charge map API or map tiles for third parties; separate API/tile policies apply. (OSMF page last edited 2021-08-10; current OSM page publication date not stated; both accessed 2026-10-14.) [OSMF, Licence](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence); [OpenStreetMap, Copyright and License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

- The open-source F1DB repository is a useful example of why a dataset license is not the same thing as a rights clearance for a real circuit. Its current README claims it includes **“All circuits (including layouts and SVG assets)”** and states: **“F1DB is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License.”** The repository page displays a CC-BY-4.0 license and was current in 2026. CC BY 4.0 permits sharing/adaptation with attribution and change indication, but the CC deed warns that the license may not provide all permissions needed and that other rights, including trademark, privacy, publicity, and moral rights, may limit use. (Repository/README publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [F1DB repository and README](https://github.com/f1db/f1db); [Creative Commons, CC BY 4.0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

### Inferences

- **Physical circuit vs. expression:** the existence, location, broad function, and publicly observable dimensions of a circuit may be factual or functional in a particular jurisdiction, but a specific drawn outline, CAD file, map rendering, SVG path, database selection/arrangement, elevation profile, or game mesh is an expressive/data artifact that can carry separate rights. This follows from the expression/facts distinction plus WIPO’s treatment of maps, technical drawings, architecture, and databases as potentially protected. [WIPO, Copyright](https://www.wipo.int/copyright/en/); [U.S. Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html)

- An **independently authored exact recreation** is safer than copying a file or ripping a mesh, but it is not automatically risk-free: F1’s own guidance signals that circuit outlines may be owned by the circuit owner, and a highly recognizable recreation can still raise circuit-owner copyright, database, design, contract, or unfair-association issues even if the artist used public observations rather than an official file. [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- ODbL can make OSM a legally usable source for geographic context, but it does not certify that every underlying feature is free of all third-party rights. It also does not license trademarks or the independent copyright in contents. Use of OSM is therefore a **source-compliance task**, not a blanket clearance for reproducing a named real circuit. [OpenStreetMap, Copyright and License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); [ODbL v1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)

- A permissive repository license such as CC BY 4.0 can authorize use of that repository’s copyrightable database/asset contribution, but it cannot be assumed to waive circuit-owner, trademark, publicity, moral-rights, contractual, or other rights. F1DB’s own scope statement (“including layouts and SVG assets”) makes it a high-risk source for an exact game track, not a reason to ship the assets without additional review. [F1DB](https://github.com/f1db/f1db); [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

### Gaps

- No authoritative source located in this review states a universal rule or court holding that a Formula 1 circuit’s physical centerline/outline is always copyrightable or always uncopyrightable. The answer varies by jurisdiction and by what was copied: facts/coordinates, a technical drawing, a database, an original artistic rendering, a CAD file, or a 3D game asset.

- The review did not identify the rights holder and current licence terms for each individual F1 circuit. Formula 1’s current guidance itself directs circuit-outline licensing enquiries to the circuit IP owner; exact recreations would require a circuit-by-circuit clearance exercise in the intended distribution countries. [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

## 2. Trademarks, Formula 1/FIA rights, and game assets

### Takeaway

Removing F1 names, logos, branding, official assets, and trademarks removes a major and obvious risk, but it does not create a blanket safe harbour for an exact track recreation. The current Formula 1 guidance is unusually direct: its logos require an express written licence; its word marks cannot brand a game; its other championship IP cannot be used in third-party games; and simulators/software simulating auto racing should not use Formula 1 Rights without an express licence. Real circuit names, team/driver/sponsor marks, official typefaces, timing data, signs, liveries, screenshots, sounds, and ripped code are separate clearance problems.

### Cited Findings

- WIPO defines a trademark as **“a sign capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one enterprise from those of other enterprises.”** It says registration generally confers an exclusive right to use the registered trademark and that marks can include words, letters, numerals, drawings, symbols, three-dimensional features, sounds, and colors. (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [WIPO, Trademarks](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trademarks)

- Formula 1’s current guidelines say: **“As a rule our trade marks cannot be used by third parties without a specific written licence from us.”** They list F1, Formula 1, Formula One, FIA Formula One World Championship, Grand Prix, and related marks; they say the logos cannot be used for any purpose or medium without an express written licence. (Page current as of October 2026; accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines — Our Marks](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- The same guidelines say permitted word marks may be used editorially to inform/report rather than brand, but the page’s specific Games rule says: **“Our Permitted Word Marks cannot be used to brand any game. Other Intellectual Property Rights including those from the Formula 1 companies’ official games cannot be used in third party games.”** It further says motorsport simulators/software simulating auto racing should not use Formula 1 Rights without an express written licence. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines — Games and Simulators](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- Formula 1’s guidelines identify its official typefaces/fonts as copyright-protected, prohibit copying/reproduction of its audio-visual content, and say third-party names and trademarks used with motorsport simulators should not be reproduced without the relevant rights holder’s consent. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines — Typefaces, Audio/Visual Content, Simulators](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- Formula 1’s legal notices name the relevant core marks as trademarks of Formula One Licensing BV and say all site material is protected by copyright, database rights, trademarks, and/or other IP rights. The page gives no general permission to turn site maps, data, photos, code, or other material into game content. (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Legal Notices](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/legal-notices.7egvZU48hzrypubGBNcQKt)

- Formula 1’s guidelines say that if an unofficial website uses Formula 1 marks, it must make clear that it is unofficial and not associated with the Formula 1 companies; they also prohibit use that implies affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship, or support. The guidance is not a substitute for permission, and the game/simulator rules are stricter than ordinary editorial reporting. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines — Fundamental Principles](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- Formula 1’s guidelines say rights in teams and drivers should be cleared directly with the respective teams/management and circuit IP rights with the circuit owner. Thus removing F1 branding does not clear a real circuit name, a team/driver identity, sponsor logo, livery, or circuit-specific asset. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines — Other IP rights](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- The F1 legal-notices page’s educational section allows limited copying for private study/research and says students may incorporate Formula1.com material into educational projects only where the projects are not reproduced and are not commercial; it also says material must not be modified/adapted and excludes moving images. The same page separately says site content is for personal, non-commercial use and restricts distribution, copying, display, modification, adaptation, and derivative works. (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Legal Notices — Education and Use](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/legal-notices.7egvZU48hzrypubGBNcQKt)

- The F1 guidelines say limited educational use of other Formula 1 IP **“may be acceptable where the use is justified, limited, and non-commercial,”** but expressly add that this does not include public postings such as YouTube, websites, and social media and must be private educational use. A public college technical-fest build therefore should not be planned around that narrow wording. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines — Educational use](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- The current ODbL text says trademarks associated with a database are outside its scope. This means even compliant OSM/F1DB attribution would not authorize use of F1, FIA, circuit, team, driver, or sponsor marks. (ODbL v1.0; accessed 2026-10-14.) [ODbL v1.0, section 2.3(c)](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)

### Inferences

- **No names/logos is necessary but not sufficient.** It substantially lowers trademark and false-endorsement exposure, but an exact, recognizable circuit can still be treated as copied circuit IP or as an unauthorized association, and assets/data can have independent copyright/database rights. This is the practical effect of reading F1’s Games/Simulators guidance together with its circuit-outline guidance and the copyright sources above. [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt); [WIPO, Copyright](https://www.wipo.int/copyright/en/)

- “Non-commercial college event” reduces commercial-exploitation and market-substitution concerns, but it does not automatically authorize reproduction, public display, distribution, public download, or use of a protected game asset. The current F1 wording distinguishes private educational use from public postings, and UK IPO guidance treats exceptions as fact-specific and limited. [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt); [GOV.UK / IPO, Exceptions](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright)

- A disclaimer can reduce confusion but cannot grant copyright, database, trademark, or contract permission. The cleanest risk reduction is to avoid the marks and avoid making the game recognizably an official or licensed F1 product in the first place; do not use “unofficial F1” as a branding strategy. [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

### Gaps

- This review did not search every country’s trademark register for every F1 circuit name, team, sponsor, or driver. Names and marks are territorial and can involve registration, passing-off/unfair-competition, personality/publicity, or venue-specific contractual restrictions.

- No current FIA page located in this review provides a general public licence for third-party college games to use FIA/F1 marks, circuit homologation diagrams, or FIA technical data. Treat FIA-hosted documents as reference/regulatory material, not as an asset licence, unless the document itself grants a compatible licence.

## 3. Conservative engineering recommendation, attribution records, and ship gate

### Takeaway

For a technical-fest game, ship a fictional championship with **original track geometry and original game assets**. Use public sources only for general design research or properly licensed geographic context. Treat an exact recreation of a real F1 circuit as a separate licensing project: if exactness is a required feature, obtain written permission from the circuit/IP owner and clear any F1/series/team/driver/sponsor rights before public demonstration or distribution. Do not rely on “we renamed it,” “we used public maps,” “it is educational,” or “we charge nothing” as the clearance record.

### Cited Findings

- Formula 1’s guidelines direct circuit-outline licensing enquiries to the circuit IP owner and separately restrict F1 marks, official game IP, typefaces, assets, timing data, audio/visual material, and simulator uses. This supports treating exact-layout clearance and branding clearance as separate workstreams. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

- WIPO says the right holder can authorize or prevent reproduction and adaptation, and says licenses may authorize use under specified conditions. It also says protected works on the Internet generally remain protected unless a licence, limitation, exception, or public-domain status applies. (Publication date not stated; accessed 2026-10-14.) [WIPO, Copyright](https://www.wipo.int/copyright/en/); [WIPO, Copyright FAQ](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright)

- OSM requires a visible credit to OpenStreetMap and its contributors and a notice/link to ODbL; the ODbL legal text requires license/notice handling for a publicly conveyed derivative database and a source notice for a publicly used Produced Work. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [OpenStreetMap, Copyright and License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); [ODbL v1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)

- CC BY 4.0 requires appropriate credit, a license link, and indication of changes; its deed says the license does not necessarily grant trademark, publicity, privacy, or moral-rights permission. (Accessed 2026-10-14.) [Creative Commons, CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

- UK IPO says the creator normally owns commissioned work absent a written agreement and that student ownership depends on the institution’s contract. For a college team, authorship and permission records should therefore cover student-created geometry, meshes, textures, UI, audio, and code before the fest build is publicly exhibited or uploaded. (Published 2014-08-19; accessed 2026-10-14.) [GOV.UK / IPO, Ownership of copyright works](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works)

### Inferences

#### Recommended build profile: lowest practical risk

1. **Geometry:** author a new centerline and track envelope from generic requirements such as target lap length, width, corner-radius range, elevation limits, braking zones, and surface types. Use a fictional location/name. Do not trace an F1 circuit outline or preserve the same distinctive turn sequence, proportions, landmark placement, or exact coordinates. This is an engineering risk-control recommendation based on the expression/facts distinction and F1’s circuit-outline notice, not a legal test. [WIPO, Copyright](https://www.wipo.int/copyright/en/); [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

2. **Research inputs:** public racing videos/articles can inform generic physics/design concepts, but do not import official maps, CAD/homologation drawings, screenshots, game data, telemetry/timing data, or track meshes. If geographic context is genuinely needed, use an ODbL-compliant OSM extract or another source with a written licence; do not treat an OSM licence as a licence to copy an exact F1 circuit. [OpenStreetMap, Copyright and License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); [ODbL v1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/); [Formula 1, Legal Notices](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/legal-notices.7egvZU48hzrypubGBNcQKt)

3. **Assets:** make original signs, fictional sponsors, fictional series/team names, original liveries, original UI, original type choices, original sounds, and original screenshots. Do not reuse F1’s official typeface, logos, graphics, textures, audio, images, timing data, or any code/asset extracted from an official or fan game. [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt); [Formula 1, Legal Notices](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/legal-notices.7egvZU48hzrypubGBNcQKt)

4. **Public-facing identity:** use a distinct game title, fictional championship, fictional circuit name, and fictional visual identity. Do not put F1, Formula 1, Formula One, FIA Formula One World Championship, Grand Prix, circuit names, team/driver names, or sponsor marks in the game title, menu, store/package metadata, promotional poster, or track signage unless separately cleared. [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt); [WIPO, Trademarks](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trademarks)

#### Source and rights ledger to keep with the project

For every external input or contributor, keep a machine-readable ledger and a human-readable `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` containing:

- source URL/repository and exact file/path;
- access/download date (UTC), release/tag/commit, and a local licence-text snapshot;
- copyright/author/contributor and stated rights holder;
- licence identifier and the exact permissions relevant to this use: modification, public display, public download, redistribution, game use, commercial/non-commercial scope, attribution, share-alike, notice, and no-endorsement terms;
- whether the source contains third-party content or separate trademarks/rights not covered by the repository licence;
- SHA-256 hashes of raw inputs and shipped outputs, plus a short transformation note showing what the team authored or changed;
- student/contractor contribution agreements or licences covering geometry, 3D meshes, textures, UI, audio, documentation, and code, checked against the college’s IP policy; and
- a removal/replace plan if a source licence cannot be verified.

This ledger is an engineering provenance control and evidence of good-faith diligence; it is not itself a licence. The need to record student ownership and written assignments/licences follows the UK IPO ownership guidance, and the need to retain licence/attribution details follows ODbL and CC BY terms. [GOV.UK / IPO](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works); [ODbL v1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/); [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

#### Attribution/notice examples

- If OSM data materially informs a shipped map or context layer, include an in-game credits/readme notice naming **OpenStreetMap and its contributors**, linking to `https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright` and `https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/`, and follow the relevant OSM attribution guidance. If a derivative database is distributed, preserve the ODbL licence/notice and share-alike obligations. [OpenStreetMap, Copyright and License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); [ODbL v1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)

- If any F1DB material were ever used, follow the repository’s CC BY 4.0 attribution and indicate changes, while separately clearing any rights not covered by that licence. The conservative recommendation for this fest is **do not ship F1DB circuit SVG/layout assets**: use an independently authored track instead. [F1DB](https://github.com/f1db/f1db); [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

- For student-created content, list contributors/owners according to their agreements and the college policy. Do not label a project “official,” “licensed,” “endorsed,” or “in partnership with” F1/FIA/circuit owners without written authority. [GOV.UK / IPO](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works); [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

#### Ship/no-ship gate

**Do not ship or publicly demonstrate without clearance:**

- an exact real-circuit outline/centerline/mesh, especially one traced from an official map/CAD/homologation plan, official game, F1DB layout/SVG, or an unverified fan dataset;
- official F1/FIA/series/team/driver/sponsor names, logos, liveries, signs, marks, event titles, typeface, or visual identity;
- official race timing/results/statistics/telemetry or scraped site/database content where the source licence does not clearly cover public game use;
- screenshots, photographs, videos, audio, textures, code, or 3D assets copied/ripped from Formula 1’s websites, broadcasts, official games, or other games;
- OSM-derived data without the required attribution/licence notices, or raw/derivative database distribution that does not satisfy ODbL; and
- any dataset whose licence, provenance, or third-party rights cannot be verified.

These are conservative project controls, not findings that each listed item is necessarily infringing in every jurisdiction. [Formula 1, Legal Notices](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/legal-notices.7egvZU48hzrypubGBNcQKt); [Formula 1, Guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt); [OpenStreetMap, Copyright and License](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); [ODbL v1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)

**Decision rule:** if the requirement is “the player must recognize this as the exact real circuit,” pause the build and obtain written permission from the circuit/IP owner and any relevant series/brand rights holder before public use. If the requirement is “teach racing physics and track design,” proceed with original geometry and fictional branding; retain the provenance ledger and include only the attributions actually triggered by licensed sources.

### Gaps

- The exact permissions for a specific circuit, map provider, track-data repository, college, or country were not established here. Before a public release, identify the intended jurisdictions, the circuit(s), the distribution method (in-person only, downloadable build, web build, public repository), and the college’s IP/insurance/takedown policy.

- The source review did not provide a jurisdiction-specific opinion on whether a particular independently redrawn circuit outline would infringe. A local IP professional should review any plan that still aims to recreate a real circuit exactly or nearly exactly.

- Access-date record for all dynamic pages in this note: **2026-10-14**. Source publication dates are only stated where the page exposed them: GOV.UK ownership (2014-08-19), GOV.UK exceptions (2014-06-12; updated 2021-01-04), OSMF licence revision (2021-08-10), and Formula 1’s current guidelines event-title list (October 2026). All other “current” web pages should be rechecked immediately before release because their terms can change.
